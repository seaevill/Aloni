**Keywords** (for the subtitle, Apple's 100-character keyword field and Google's 80-character short description):
- Greek: ψεκασμός, βιβλίο/ημερολόγιο ψεκασμών, καιρός ψεκασμού, σπορά, χωράφι, αγρότης, κλούβες.
- Greeklish: psekasmos, agrotis.
- English: spray log, spray record, farm diary, crop planner.
- Turkish (later): ilaçlama kaydı, tarla defteri.

Strongest term before the 1 Jan 2027 e-record deadline: "Βιβλίο Ψεκασμών".

**Ranking**
1. **Stremma.** It is the unit every Greek grower uses, and no app uses it as a name, so a brand search finds only us. Easy to say in Turkish, Albanian, Bulgarian and Arabic, and the Latin and Greek spellings match. Weak point: it's a common word, so the subtitle has to carry the keywords. Editorial story: "a Megara grower built his own app".
2. **Avlaki.** It has the most character and is easy to own in search. The Latin spelling matches how it is said. Some people won't know the word.
3. **Klouva.** One family brand with Κλούβες, which matches our differentiator (orders → sowing). Non-Greeks may say "klo-oo-va". If the two apps merge, "Klouves" is the better umbrella name.
4. **Chorafi.** It sounds warm, but "χωράφι" search results are flooded with land-for-sale listings. Latin readers say "ch" as in "chore". The spelling "Horafi" sounds like the Turkish *hurafe* ("superstition"), which is bad for an app that gives weather advice.
5. **Tetradio.** Fits the notebook story, but results are full of school and note apps.

**Previously floated names**
- **SunField:** avoid. Results are full of solar companies (Sunfield Solar LLC, Shenzhen Sunfield New Energy, Sunfields PV), and the name means nothing to Greek farmers. Fine as a code name.
- **Kloubes Agros:** two words, and the "b" is misread ("kloo-bez"). Klouva gives the same family link more cleanly.

**Apple guideline risks**
- 2.3.7: no keyword stuffing, and no agency or competitor names (e.g. ΟΠΕΚΕΠΕ) in the metadata.
- 2.3.1: no "certified" or "official" claims. "Βιβλίο Ψεκασμών" is fine; "εγκεκριμένο" ("approved") is not.
- 4.2: a wrapped PWA needs native value (push, offline use) to pass review.
- 5.2: Klouva is clean because Bora owns Κλούβες. The others are common nouns, so they are hard to block and also hard to register: register the word together with a logo.
